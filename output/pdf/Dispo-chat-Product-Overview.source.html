01  The case
The problem

Dispatch runs on tools that were
never built for dispatch.

A transport office coordinates trucks, drivers, customers and partners in real time — and does it across four apps that know nothing about each other.

The thread is everywhere

WhatsApp for the driver, email for the customer, a phone call to the yard, a spreadsheet for the plan. No load ever has one home.

Context dies with the message

A plate, a CMR photo, a delivery window, a price — buried in an untyped thread with no structure, no status and nothing to search.

The truck is a black box

Status lives in someone's head. The answer to "where is it?" is another phone call, and it goes stale the moment it's given.

Each of these is a conversation problem before it is a software problem — which is why bolting a chat tab onto a TMS has never fixed it.

The case
03
01  The case
The idea
Dispo-chat is built on one idea

Every conversation is attached to
a real transport object.

Open a room in Dispo-chat and you are not opening a thread — you are opening a truck. Its plates, its driver, its current status, its trip, every stop on that trip, the documents proving each one, and the people responsible. The chat is the interface; the operation is the record. Nothing has to be re-entered somewhere else to count.

Chat-first

People already coordinate by message. The product meets that habit instead of fighting it — then makes the messages structured.

Operationally typed

A room has a type. A vehicle room carries status, a trip, stops and proofs that the interface understands and can act on.

A network, not a silo

Many carriers share the platform. Inside your company chat is open; across companies it is gated by an explicit handshake.

Tenancy & trust

One workspace per company.
One network between them.

Signing up creates a company workspace and its first admin. Everything — people, vehicles, places, conversations — is scoped to that workspace and gated by membership on every single query.

Inside the workspace

Open by default
  • Colleagues are trusted contacts — no connection request needed to DM anyone in your own company.
  • A live Company roster in the sidebar, kept in sync over a workspace-wide socket room.
  • Members join by email invite with a pre-assigned role, or by an invite link.
  • Shared saved places — depots, customers, borders — reusable across every trip.
Explicit
handshake

Across companies

Gated by consent
  • A company directory to find carriers and partners already on the platform.
  • Request → accept → decline, LinkedIn-style, with realtime notification on every step.
  • A cross-workspace DM is impossible until a connection is accepted — enforced server-side, not in the UI.
  • Public company profiles so a partner knows who they're accepting.

03  Conversations
Room types

A room is a thing, not a thread.

Two room types are live today, and they behave differently because they mean different things.

Vehicle rooms

A truck and everything happening to it

The centre of the product. Created from the workspace with tractor and trailer plates, then it accumulates the operation.

  • Vehicle — plates, capacity, notes, current status.
  • Trip — order reference, client, route, and a status walked through 15 stages.
  • Stops — typed waypoints with windows, addresses and completion state.
  • Members — dispatchers plus the assigned driver, each with a room role.
  • Documents — the transport paperwork the load is expected to produce.

Direct messages

People, inside and outside the company

One-to-one, with the trust boundary built into the room itself rather than bolted on as a setting.

  • Same workspace — open immediately, no request, no ceremony.
  • Different workspace — requires an accepted connection; the server refuses to create the room otherwise.
  • Profile preview — tap any avatar for the person's role, company and contact detail.
  • Photo identity — DMs show the person; vehicle rooms show a truck glyph, so the list is scannable at a glance.

The message toolkit

Everything you expect from a
modern messenger — and it all persists.

Not a demo chat. Edits, deletes, pins and forwards are real endpoints with real broadcasts, membership-gated on the server.

Reply & quote

Threaded context inline, with a tap back to the original.

Edit & delete

Edit history flagged; deletes tombstoned, never silently vanished.

Pinned messages

A pinned bar above the thread for the reference everyone keeps asking for.

Forward

Push a message into any other room you're a member of.

Mentions

@people and @trips, stored as first-class rows with their own badge count.

Scheduled send

Write it now, deliver at a chosen time — a server worker does the sending.

Attachments

Drag, paste or pick. Images and PDFs preview before they send.

Search

In-conversation find with highlight-and-jump, over a dedicated index.

Read receipts

Per-member, with a panel showing exactly who has seen it and when.

System messages

Status changes, joins and assignments narrated into the thread itself.

Drafts

Per-room, kept locally, restored when you come back to the room.

Two display modes

Bubbles or a plain stream; compact rows or avatar rows. Your call.

History is cursor-paginated off a dedicated (group_id, created_at desc) index, so scrolling back through a year of a truck's life stays fast — and messages de-duplicate by id, so a reconnect never doubles the thread.

03  Conversations
Presence & liveness

Nothing needs a refresh.

Dispatch is a real-time job. Twenty-seven distinct events travel over a single authenticated socket, so what one person changes, everyone sees happen.

Presence

Who is online right now, snapshotted on connect and patched on change — with an idle detector so "online" means it.

Typing

Relayed per room, throttled, and shown in the header as well as the thread.

Unread & mentions

Denormalised counters, so the rail badge is a read, not an aggregate scan.

Notifications

Web Push to the device, browser notifications on desktop, plus an optional sound.

Reconnection is handled by the transport with backoff, and every payload carries an id — so a tunnel, a lift or a dead zone costs you latency, never a message.

Vehicle state

The truck has a status.
So does everyone's understanding of it.

Vehicle state is set by hand, on purpose. It is what a dispatcher knows — not what a tracker guesses — and it shows up everywhere the vehicle appears.

Vehicle status
AvailableDriving LoadingUnloading WaitingBreak ServiceCompleted

One tone, everywhere

A status colour is defined once and reused by the sidebar row, the chat header, the trip strip and the fleet board. Nothing drifts.

Vehicle record

Tractor and trailer plates, capacity and free-form notes live on the room and drive the truck-legal routing profile.

Assigned drivers

The driver assignment is what unlocks the mobile trip view and gates every driver endpoint on the server.

A deliberate constraint. Nothing about vehicle or trip status is inferred from GPS. Location is shown when a driver's phone reports it, but it never silently rewrites a status a human set — the operational record stays something a person is accountable for.

04  Transport operations
The trip

Fifteen stages, in the order a load actually moves.

Not "open / closed". The lifecycle names the states a European road load really passes through, so a glance at the rail answers the question without a phone call.

Planned→ Accepted→ Going to loading→ At loading→ Loaded→ In transit→ At customs→ Ferry
Break→ Service→ Going to unloading→ At unloading→ Unloaded→ Completed or Cancelled
Stops

A trip is a list of typed waypoints, each with an address, a time window, and its own completion state — planned, done or cancelled.

LoadingUnloading CustomsFerryFuel ServiceParkingBreak Other

Addresses are picked from HERE search or from the workspace's saved places, so the same depot is the same coordinates every time.

Proof of transport

Before a driver leaves a loading stop — or completes an unloading one — the app asks for the paperwork.

  • JPEG, PNG or PDF, up to 25 MB, checked server-side.
  • Written to an append-only table, tied to the trip, the stop, the kind and the uploader.
  • Never edited, never overwritten — an audit trail, not a file field.

Progress at a glance

Completed stops over total, drawn in the status tone, on the strip above every vehicle conversation.

Files & recall

Paper is the product.

Transport runs on documents — CMR, POD, transport orders, delivery notes, invoices. Attachments are treated as infrastructure, not a nice-to-have.

Send anything

Drag onto the room, paste, or pick from the attach menu — with a caption bar to say what it is before it goes.

Previews that render

Images get a generated preview; PDFs render to a real first-page thumbnail and open inline, without a download.

Off the request path

The upload stores, posts and returns. Image work runs as a queued job and the preview arrives over the socket when it's ready.

Private by default

Bytes stream through the API, gated on room membership. A file URL is not a capability — there are no public object links.

Identity & the network

Who you are is part of the product.

In a network where strangers do business, a profile is not decoration — it is the thing a partner decides on. Five profile surfaces share one visual language and one permission rule.

My profile

Photo, name, role, contact detail and availability.

User preview

The same surface, read-only, for anyone else you meet.

Company profile

Legal name, fleet size, lanes and dispatch contact.

Account

Sign-in, verification and the reference for all five.

Workspace settings

Members, roles, invites, places and appearance.

Identity you can rely on

  • Display name, e-mail and company name are immutable after signup — you cannot become someone else after a partner has connected with you.
  • Legal name and dispatch e-mail lock once set, so they can be filled in later but not quietly swapped.
  • Enforced server-side on every write, not just greyed out in the interface.
  • E-mail addresses are verified by token before an account is trusted.

Getting people in

  • Workspace invites — e-mail a colleague with the role already chosen; they land straight into a registration form bound to that invite.
  • Group invites — bring a partner into a single vehicle room without giving them the rest of your workspace.
  • Connection requests — the cross-company handshake, with an inbox for pending requests.

06  Under the hood
Scale & operations

Built to add a second instance,
not to need one on day one.

The realtime layer is the part that breaks quietly when you scale a chat app. It was designed for that first, and the switch is a single environment variable.

Multi-instance fan-out

Sockets pin to one instance. Without a shared adapter, an event emitted on A never reaches a member on B — and it fails silently.

  • Redis adapter wired through every realtime path, so there are no call-site exceptions.
  • Membership changes fan out too — a user's sockets join and leave rooms wherever they live.
  • Unset in dev: in-memory. Set in prod: shared — and it refuses to boot if it can't connect.

Query & index discipline

The message table grows without limit, so paging it is a first-order concern.

  • A full (group_id, created_at desc) index keeps cursor pagination index-backed, tombstones and all.
  • Redundant indexes dropped to cut write amplification on the hot path.
  • Unread counts denormalised — the rail badge is a read, not an aggregate.
  • A documented concurrent index build path for already-large tables.

Observability

Structured JSON lines on the paths that actually tell you something when it's slow — and never a message body, cookie, token or file byte.

  • http_request — route, status, duration, user and group.
  • preview_job — status and duration for every image job.
  • socket_connect / disconnect — with a live connection count.
  • redis_adapter_* — the scaling posture is visible at boot, not inferred.
Known and tracked

The preview queue is in-process today — jobs queued at shutdown are lost, which is exactly why a re-runnable backfill command exists. A durable driver sits behind a config flag and would change one file.

Security & data

A multi-tenant chat app is a
permission system with a text box.

Companies that compete with each other share this database. Isolation is not a feature of the interface; it is a property of every query.

Authentication

Stateless JWT in an httpOnly, sameSite cookie, secure in production. Passwords hashed with bcrypt. The unused session table was dropped rather than left lying around.

Authorization

Every read and write is scoped by workspace or room membership in the query itself. Driver endpoints are gated twice — member and assigned — and answer 403 or 404, never data.

Input & abuse

Zod validation on every payload. Rate limits on sign-in, message creation, room creation and uploads. Upload size and MIME type checked on the server, not the client.

Deletion without destruction

Users are anonymised, never physically deleted — a departed colleague's messages stay readable in context for the people still working the load, without their personal data surviving.

Attachment access

No public object URLs. Bytes are streamed through a membership-gated route with immutable cache headers, so a leaked link is not a leaked file.

Honest failure

Unmatched API paths answer as the API, never as the app — so a renamed endpoint returns a 404 instead of an HTML page a client cheerfully decodes as corrupt data.

Still open, and named as such: a CSRF review for state-changing requests, a global one-account-per-e-mail identity model before public launch, anti-abuse limits on connection requests, and a scripted test harness for the auth path.

What's next

The next loop is the network.

Internal dispatch works. The unfinished half is the reason a company would rather be here than in a group chat — doing business with the company next door.

Near

Close the network loop

Discover a company, connect, and trade.

  • A searchable company directory with public profiles as the front door.
  • Room-level document filing — CMR, POD, invoices — on the existing upload pipeline.
  • The global one-account-per-e-mail identity model, before public launch.
  • Anti-abuse on connection requests: limits, blocking, reporting.
Then

The commercial object

Turn the conversation into a transaction.

  • Shipment and quote rooms alongside vehicle rooms — price, currency, expiry, status.
  • Milestones from quoted to invoiced, so a load has a commercial life, not just a physical one.
  • Full-text search across rooms, references, plates, companies and places.
  • Filters by status, date, customer, partner and vehicle.
Ongoing

Production hardening

Named, tracked, unglamorous.

  • Durable job queue behind the flag that's already reserved for it.
  • Log shipping and error monitoring, then latency and failure-rate alerts.
  • CDN-fronted attachments to move egress off the app instances.
  • CI for build, lint, migrations and tests; a scripted auth harness.
  • Backups, retention and data export decided before external users, not after.

The roadmap is kept in the repository and reconciled against the code, so an unfinished item is visible in the plan rather than implied by its absence from the app.
